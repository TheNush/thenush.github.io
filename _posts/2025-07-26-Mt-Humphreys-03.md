The constant cold wind had my nose running. My quads were beginning to burn from steeper elevation and larger rocks. My lungs were hanging in there thanks to my slower pace. 
My heart was drowning in misery as I had just passed the second false peak. I'm sure the rest of my body was feeling this "extra weight". 
It felt like we had been climbing forever -- certainly well past the 5mi limit on the one-way trial. 
After two false peaks, I no longer trusted the upcoming summit. This uncertainty about the lenght of the hike made it much more challenging to stay focused on the present as I'd done 
up until that point. An experience of suffering grows ever more painful when one starts to question its finiteness. Often, the finiteness of suffering is used as motivation to keep pushing. 
You're at the gym lifting weights and you think, "Just one more set" or "Three more reps, come on!". There is a definite end in sight and that pushes us to fight harder. But what happens when 
that finish line is not so apparent? How do we find strength to keep going? What if you were stuck on set two of the third exercise of your day's workout and never got to proceed? 
The very thought terrifies me, as I'm sure it would most people. Fortunately, I had heard other hikers mention that there were only two false peaks (yeah, where were these people before the 
first false peak!) and that this truly was the summit of Mt. Humphreys. As I appraoched, I noticed the trailmarker demarkating Humphreys peak -- an elevation of 12,633ft -- and began to feel 
my heart lighten giving me an almost immediate surge of energy. It was a matter of minutes before I summitted Mt. Humphreys! My first major hike -- done! 

At the top, I finally took in the panaromic view that stretched across in all directions to the horizon and it was stunning. Mt. Humphreys being the tallest peak in the state of Arizona meant we 
had nothing blocking our vision in every direction we looked. We could see the city of Flagstaff to our south and a little glimpse of the rim of the Grand Canyon to our north! It was an absolute 
treat for the eyes and certainly worth the arduous trek. As I sat down trying to make myself comfortable, I took a moment to acknowledge the mountain itself; in some sense, paying my respects to 
its strength and valor. We had had our duel and now the game neared its end. In that moment, I was grateful -- to the others in the group for inviting me and the wonderful company, to the 
encouraging and friendly hikers along the way, and finally to the mountain for standing the test of time and waiting for me. It was all so sureal and satisfying. 

Sitting atop the tallest peak for at least a few hundred miles, contemplating life, grateful for this wonderful experience, I noticed something crawling up my shoulder. 
I dusted it off quickly only to find a couple more on my other shoulder. 
My friend then pointed out to me that there were a few in my hair -- Eww!! I ruffled my hair briskly, icked by the feeling of them coming off. There was a massive swarm of bugs in the area. 
They seemed to look similar to fruit flies (I later found out that they were gnats!) and so didn't panic too much. Needless to say, I was certainly disturbed by their prescence. I figured I could
avoid them more if I wasn't a sitting target. I began walking around when I noticed one of the guys from our group trying to take a picture of a raven(?), high above us, surfing a wave of wind. 
Here we were, at the top of a mountain that was more than 12,600 ft, feeling great about what we had accomplished. Yet, there is this bird for whom being at this height and altitude is just another 
Monday. Really puts things in perspective.
